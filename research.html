Research

My work sits at the intersection of hardware design and AI security — building efficient accelerators for deep learning models and hardening them against adversarial threats.

Hardware Acceleration

Hardware-Software Co-Design for Efficient ViT Inference

Jan 2023 – Sept 2024 · IIT Jodhpur (Advisor: Dr. Palash Das)

Vision Transformers (ViTs) achieve state-of-the-art accuracy but are computationally expensive. This research addresses the challenge of deploying ViTs efficiently on dedicated hardware through informative patch/token selection and custom accelerator design.

Key Contributions
  • Proposed lightweight, image-adaptive informative patch selection techniques for efficient yet accurate ViT acceleration.
  • Designed and implemented a dedicated ASIC (HAVIT) to accelerate both patch selection and the full ViT pipeline.
  • Analyzed token selector placement in the ViT architecture, optimizing the accuracy–computation trade-off in attention-based methods.
  • Proposed a feature-based token pruning strategy for early layers, reducing reliance on attention scores.
  • Developed a hybrid multi-stage token pruning methodology, reducing execution time by 29–40% with minimal accuracy loss.
  • Implemented hardware accelerator for ViT backbone on FPGA using Vitis HLS and Vivado.
Outcomes: HAVIT accepted in ACM Transactions on Architecture and Code Optimization (ACM TACO), 2025. Hybrid token selector accepted as a regular paper at DATE 2025, IEEE.
AI Security

Adversarial Robustness of Vision Transformers

August 2025 – present · IIT Guwahati

Adversarial attacks pose a serious threat to the real-world deployment of deep learning models. This line of research develops both attack methods and defenses specifically targeting ViTs, with a focus on scenarios where models are optimized for efficient inference on custom hardware.

Key Contributions
  • Developed model-agnostic defenses for adversarial patch attacks on Vision Transformers (STRAP-ViT).
  • Analyzed efficiency-targeted adversarial attacks that exploit input-adaptive optimization strategies in ViTs.
  • Proposed approaches to enhance robustness of optimized/accelerated ViT inference pipelines.
  • Investigating stronger energy-based attacks on efficient AI inference hardware.
  • Working on improving robustness of large language models against jailbreak prompts.
Outcomes: STRAP-ViT accepted at Design Automation Conference (DAC), 2026. Survey on adversarial efficiency degradation accepted at AIIoT 2025.
Secure Hardware

Secure Hardware Accelerator for Resilience Against Adversarial Attacks

May 2024 – July 2025 · IIT Jodhpur (Advisor: Dr. Palash Das)

This project bridges hardware design and AI security by building a dedicated FPGA-based accelerator that can detect and neutralize adversarial noise in real time, enabling secure ViT deployment in resource-constrained edge settings.

Key Contributions
  • Developing a lightweight rectifier algorithm to eliminate adversarial noise from ViT inputs.
  • Implementing a custom hardware accelerator on FPGA for real-time rectification.
  • Evaluating system performance under white-box and black-box attack scenarios.
Outcomes: Manuscript in final rounds of review at IEEE Transactions on Computers (TC).
Computer Architecture

Reinforcement Learning-Based Cache Prefetcher Design

May 2024 – May 2025 · IIT Jodhpur (Advisors: Dr. Dip Sankar Banerjee & Dr. Palash Das)

Cache prefetching is critical for memory system performance. This project improves RL-based prefetchers by refining the Q-value update process to increase cache coverage while reducing pollution, and estimates hardware overhead through custom implementations.

Key Contributions
  • Modified existing RL-based Q-value update process (multi-step SARSA) to improve cache coverage and reduce cache pollution.
  • Developed hardware to estimate area and power overhead of the proposed prefetcher.
Outcomes: Published in IEEE Embedded Systems Letters (ESL), 2025.
Application

Efficient Disease Prediction Using Vision AI on Blood Samples

Aug 2024 – Dec 2024 · IIT Jodhpur (Advisors: Dr. Hirshikesh & Dr. Palash Das)

An end-to-end application project combining fine-tuned ViT models for blood sample analysis with a custom FPGA hardware accelerator for deployment.

Key Contributions
  • Fine-tuned a Vision Transformer on blood sample images for accurate disease detection.
  • Developed a custom hardware accelerator to deploy the algorithm on an FPGA.
